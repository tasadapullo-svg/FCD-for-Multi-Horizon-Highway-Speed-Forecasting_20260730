Abstract
The Pan Borneo Highway is a major interregional road infrastructure system
in East Malaysia, where long-distance highway monitoring can be constrained
by sparse roadside sensing infrastructure. Floating car data (FCD) provide an
alternative source for highway speed forecasting in such environments. However,
FCD services may omit planned observation slots, obscuring the complete obser-
vation space, while model comparisons may be affected by inconsistent samples,
preprocessing boundaries, or limited evaluation periods. This study proposes
the Grid-Reconstructed, Auditable, Temporally Causal, and Eligibility-Gated
Multi-Horizon FCD Forecasting (GATE-FCD) framework for reproducible high-
way speed forecasting. GATE-FCD reconstructs the complete spatiotemporal
grid, applies eligibility gating, and constructs a 24-h temporally causal history
at 51 fixed virtual observation nodes. Before model comparison, common sam-
ple manifests are frozen across six retrospective rolling-origin folds for 1-, 3-,
and 6-h forecasting horizons (H1, H3, and H6), ensuring that all models are
evaluated under consistent data and temporal boundaries. Among the evalu-
ated models, XGBoost achieved the lowest descriptive mean absolute error at

all three horizons, with MAEs of 0.949, 0.829, and 0.752 km h −1 for H1, H3,
and H6, respectively. The results demonstrate the feasibility of auditable multi-
horizon forecasting using sparse FCD under controlled evaluation conditions.
Future research should extend the observation period, incorporate independent
reference speeds, and evaluate the framework across additional corridors and
FCD providers.
