Title
Can Sparse Floating Car Data Reliably Forecast Highway Speeds? A Multi-Horizon Evaluation of Forecasting Models

Short title
Multi-Horizon Highway Speed Forecasting from Sparse FCD

Abstract
Evaluating highway speed forecasts is challenging where fixed roadside sensing is sparse and commercial floating car data (FCD) are incomplete. When FCD are obtained through an application programming interface (API), planned observations may be omitted rather than returned as explicit missing values, while model comparisons can be biased by unequal evaluation samples or temporally non-causal preprocessing. We address these issues with the Grid-Reconstructed, Auditable, Temporally Causal, and Eligibility-Gated Multi-Horizon FCD Forecasting (GATE-FCD) framework. GATE-FCD reconstructs the planned observation grid, applies explicit eligibility rules, freezes common sample manifests, and restricts preprocessing to information available before each forecast origin. Historical Average, Seasonal Historical Average (SeasonalHA), Persistence, Ridge, XGBoost, and a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) are evaluated at 51 virtual nodes using 24-h histories, six retrospective rolling-origin folds, and 1-, 3-, and 6-h future-window targets (H1, H3, and H6). XGBoost achieved the lowest descriptive mean absolute error (MAE) at H1, H3, and H6 (0.949, 0.829, and 0.752 km h-1, respectively), although SeasonalHA remained competitive and fold-level inference did not establish a stable advantage. These findings highlight the importance of auditable sample construction and temporally causal evaluation when benchmarking forecasting models with incomplete commercial FCD.
